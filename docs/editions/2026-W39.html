A New School, a New Trail, and a Busy Week Ahead

The biggest news out of Littleton this week has nothing to do with a meeting agenda or a Zoom link — it's the formal state blessing of a project that's been years in the making. The Massachusetts School Building Authority approved the budget for the Shaker Lane Elementary School replacement project, clearing a critical hurdle for a building that has served the town for 64 years and is long past its prime. The MSBA's sign-off means the work of actually replacing the school can move from aspiration to planning in earnest, and the Shaker Lane School Building Committee, which met on September 16th, is now focused on determining what comes next. For families with kids at Shaker Lane — and for anyone who cares about the long-term health of the school district — this is the week things got real.

Honoring a Steward

While the school project commanded the headlines, the Littleton Conservation Trust offered the town a quieter but equally meaningful moment: the official opening of the Art Lazarus Trail at Long Lake Park Conservation Area. The trail is named for a volunteer who gave 33 years of conservation work to this community — a remarkable tenure that deserves to be marked with more than a ribbon. The Conservation Trust's hiking challenge is also still underway, inviting residents to get out and explore the trails that volunteers like Art Lazarus spent decades protecting. It's a good week to lace up your boots.
