The Week in Littleton · Oct 26 – Nov 1, 2026

Schools, Sewers, and a Special Town Meeting Shape Littleton's Week

*A 64-year-old school gets its clearest path yet to replacement, a new sewer system opens for business, and residents packed the middle school gym to vote — all in one week.*

Compiled by Littleton Signal across 8 sources

It was the kind of week that reminds you how much civic life a small town can sustain. On Tuesday evening, October 27, residents gathered at the Charles Forbes Kaye Gymnasium at Littleton Middle School for a Special Town Meeting, with the Select Board and Finance Committee convening jointly beforehand to set the stage. Unofficial results from that meeting have since been posted. And as if the gym weren't busy enough, that same building will host the state election polls on November 3 — meaning Russell Street is becoming the undisputed center of Littleton's civic universe this fall.

A School's Moment

The biggest running story in town took several steps forward at once. The Massachusetts School Building Authority approved the budget for the Shaker Lane elementary school building project — a critical milestone for a community trying to replace a 64-year-old building. The Shaker Lane Building Committee met October 21, the School Committee gathered October 22 at the Littleton Police Department community room, and the Shaker Lane Site Council convened October 27. That's three separate bodies, in one week, all moving the same project forward. The momentum arrived alongside welcome recognition from outside town limits. Littleton Middle School has been nominated for a National Blue Ribbon award, and Littleton High School received separate state recognition for academic performance — a reminder that even as the community debates what to build next, what's happening inside the current buildings is worth celebrating.

A 64-year-old school, a brand-new sewer system, and a federal lawsuit — Littleton is reckoning with its infrastructure all at once.

Infrastructure, Old and New

While the school conversation looks ahead, two major utility stories bookend where Littleton has already been. The town's new $45 million common sewer system — nearly two years in the making and supported by $13 million in grants — is now fully operational and already showing signs of spurring economic activity in the town center. It's the kind of project that rarely gets a parade, but probably deserves one. Less celebratory is the water department's federal lawsuit seeking to recover the $20.25 million it spent building a treatment plant to remove PFAS contamination from the Spectacle Pond drinking water supply. Meanwhile, LELWD received a $285,522 state grant to replace aging electrical equipment in Littleton and Boxborough, aimed at reducing the length and impact of power outages. And if your tap water looks a little cloudy this month, don't be alarmed — the water utility is flushing the distribution system zone by zone through October and November.

Housing and Development

The 410 Great Road affordable senior housing project, developed by Lupoli Companies, is holding a public update at the Center on Shattuck on November 3 from 12:30 to 1:30 p.m. — the same day as the state election, so plan your afternoon accordingly. The Affordable Housing Trust met October 21, and the Planning Board held sessions on both October 8 and October 22, keeping the town's development pipeline moving. The Select Board will hold a property tax classification public hearing on November 2 at Town Hall, a routine but consequential annual step that shapes how the tax burden falls across residential and commercial properties.

Land and Water

On the conservation front, the Littleton Conservation Trust officially opened the Art Lazarus Trail at Long Lake Park Conservation Area, honoring a volunteer who gave 33 years of service to conservation work in town. The Trust's Fall 2026 newsletter is also out, with guidance on swapping invasive plants for native species and a look back at the year's work. The Clean Lakes Committee met October 22, and the ongoing Littleton Trails Hiking Challenge continues to draw residents out onto the town's trail network. A Conservation Commission public meeting is scheduled for 24 Green Needles Road on November 17.
