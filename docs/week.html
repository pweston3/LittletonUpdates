The Week in Littleton · October 19–25, 2026

Town Meeting, a New School, and a Week of Big Decisions

*Littleton heads into a pivotal stretch with a Special Town Meeting, a school building milestone, and a busy civic calendar all converging at once.*

Compiled by Littleton Signal across 8 sources

It is hard to overstate how much is happening in Littleton right now. The week of October 19 brought a cascade of board meetings, a major state approval for the Shaker Lane School project, and the final preparations for a Special Town Meeting on October 27 — all while early voting opened at Town Hall and Election Day loomed just around the corner. For a town that sometimes moves quietly, this week was anything but quiet.

School on the Move

The biggest structural news of the week came from the Massachusetts School Building Authority, which approved the budget for the Shaker Lane School building project — a critical green light for a community trying to replace a 64-year-old elementary school. The Shaker Lane School Building Committee met virtually on October 21 to continue its work, and the School Committee gathered the following evening in a hybrid session at the Littleton Police Department community room. The pace of meetings reflects the scope of what's at stake: a project of this scale requires sustained coordination across multiple bodies, and this week showed all of them moving in step.

A 64-year-old school, a new sewer system, a trail opening, and a Special Town Meeting — Littleton is building toward something.

Town Meeting Week

Tuesday, October 27 is shaping up to be one of the busiest single evenings in recent Littleton civic memory. A Special Town Meeting is scheduled for 7:00 PM in the Charles Forbes Kaye Gymnasium at Littleton Middle School, preceded at 6:00 PM by a joint meeting of the Select Board and Finance Committee in the same building. The Community Preservation Committee will also convene as part of the Special Town Meeting proceedings. Residents tuning in ahead of time could catch the Town Meeting Preview Show to get their bearings. One item already telegraphed: Article 2, which would have adopted a special act establishing a Town Charter, will receive only a brief presentation — no vote is planned on that measure.

Infrastructure Milestones

While town government gears up for its big meeting, Littleton's utility infrastructure had a quietly momentous week of its own. The new Littleton Common Sewer System is now fully operational after nearly two years of construction, marked by a ribbon cutting. Meanwhile, a townwide flushing of the water distribution system began on October 21 and will continue through November — routine maintenance, but a reminder of how much runs beneath the streets. On a harder note, the Littleton Water Department has filed a federal lawsuit seeking to recover costs incurred from removing PFAS — so-called forever chemicals — from the water supply, a fight playing out in courtrooms that began long before this week and will continue long after it.

Land, Lakes, and Trails

Conservation had a moment to celebrate this week with the official opening of the Art Lazarus Trail on the Long Lake Park Conservation Area — a new recreational asset for residents who enjoy the town's natural spaces. The Clean Lakes Committee and Conservation Commission both held their regular meetings, and the Littleton Conservation Trust published its Fall 2026 newsletter, highlighting native plant initiatives and a year's worth of stewardship work. Separately, the Lupoli Companies are scheduled to present a public update on the recently approved 410 Great Road project on November 3, giving neighbors a chance to hear directly from the developer about what comes next for that site.

Votes Ahead

Early in-person voting opened October 26 at Town Hall on Shattuck Street, running from 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM. The State Election follows on November 3 at Littleton Middle School, polls open 7:00 AM to 8:00 PM. Before that, the Select Board meets November 2 and will hold a public hearing on the property tax classification — a decision that directly affects how the tax burden is distributed between residential and commercial properties heading into the new fiscal year.
