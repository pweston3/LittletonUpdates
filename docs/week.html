The Week in Littleton · October 12–18, 2026

School Deal Done, Town Meeting Looms, Water Fights Back

*A week when Littleton's biggest long-term bet got a green light, its civic calendar filled up fast, and the Water Department went on offense.*

Compiled by Littleton Signal across 8 sources

For years, parents and school officials have watched students file into a 64-year-old elementary school building that has outlived its useful life. This week, that wait moved measurably closer to an end: the Massachusetts School Building Authority approved the budget for the Shaker Lane School building project, clearing a major state-level hurdle and setting the stage for the Building Committee's next meeting on October 21st. It is the kind of milestone that rarely gets a parade, but it matters — state approval is the financial backbone of any school construction project in Massachusetts, and without it, nothing gets built.

School on the Move

The MSBA's sign-off on the Shaker Lane budget lands at a moment when Littleton's schools are drawing attention for all the right reasons: Littleton Middle School has earned a Blue Ribbon nomination, and the high school has received state recognition of its own. Those accolades reflect the work happening inside the buildings; the Shaker Lane project is about making sure the buildings themselves are worthy of that work. The Building Committee meets virtually on October 21st, and residents who want to track how the project translates from approved budget to actual construction should mark that date. Separately, the School Committee met earlier this month and the Paws on LPS group gathered on October 14th, keeping community engagement in the schools humming along.

Ten Days of Civic Life

If the school project is Littleton's long game, the next two weeks are the sprint. Early in-person voting opened on October 19th at 37 Shattuck Street. The Select Board met that same evening, and the Conservation Commission convened virtually on October 20th. The Parks and Recreation Commission meets October 21st, and the Board of Assessors gathers October 22nd. Then, on October 27th, residents are invited to a Special Town Meeting preview show before the meeting itself kicks off at 7 p.m. in the Charles Forbes Kaye Gymnasium at Littleton Middle School. Worth noting: a property tax classification public hearing is already scheduled for November 2nd, so the civic calendar does not let up after Town Meeting adjourns.

Water, in Every Sense

Littleton's water story this week runs in three directions at once. The Littleton Water Department has filed a federal lawsuit to recover costs related to removing PFAS contamination — a fight that many Massachusetts communities are waging, and one that could eventually return real money to ratepayers. Meanwhile, a townwide flushing of the water distribution system begins October 21st and runs through November; residents may notice some temporary discoloration and should plan accordingly. Layered on top of all that, a Level 2 Significant Drought has triggered outdoor watering restrictions per MassDEP, making conservation not just a good idea but a requirement. Water quality reports remain available from the department for anyone who wants the full picture.

Cameras and Trails

Two items this week sit at opposite ends of Littleton's public life, but both touch on how the town manages its shared spaces. On the surveillance front, Littleton joins four other communities that ended or moved to end contracts with Flock Safety within a span of five days; a notable detail in the reporting is that five of the six cameras the Select Board ordered powered down in May were subsequently found to be live again. That discovery raises questions the board will presumably need to address. On a lighter note, the Art Lazarus Trail in Long Lake Park Conservation Area is officially open, and the Littleton Conservation Trust's fall newsletter highlights the ongoing Trails Hiking Challenge and a focus on replacing invasive plants with native species — a quieter but persistent kind of civic stewardship.
